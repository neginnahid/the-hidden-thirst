The hidden thirst

A data center does one physical thing: it turns electricity into heat. Getting rid of that heat takes water, and it is usually the same water a town drinks.

Read the facts

Six facts

What the water is doing

  1. Heat is the only product

    Almost every watt a server draws leaves the building as heat. Large sites shed it by evaporating water into the air, so that water does not return to the system it came from. US data centers evaporated 66 billion liters this way in 2023, roughly three times the 2014 figure.1

  2. It is drinking water

    Cooling towers run on treated potable supply, because untreated water scales the pipes and breeds bacteria. In one review, 57% of the water data centers drew directly came from potable sources.2

  3. Withdrawn is not consumed

    Withdrawal is water borrowed and returned. Consumption is water gone from the watershed. Evaporative cooling is consumption, which is why a tidy-looking figure can still drain a reservoir.2

  4. Built where it is driest

    Siting follows cheap land and cheap power, and that tends to mean arid country. About a fifth of the direct water footprint of US servers falls in moderately to highly stressed watersheds, and nearly half draw their power from plants in stressed regions.3

  5. There is a second bill

    Air cooling still spends water, just somewhere else: thermal power stations evaporate it to generate the electricity. Every kilowatt hour a US data center uses carries 4.52 liters of water at the power plant.1

  6. Nobody has to tell you

    Consumption figures are routinely withheld as commercially sensitive. Fewer than a third of operators measure their water use at all.2

United States, 2023

The bill you see, and the one you don’t

  • Indirect, at the power stations ~800 bn L

  • Direct, on-site cooling, 2023 66 bn L

  • Direct, on-site cooling, 2014 21.2 bn L

On-site use tripled in nine years and it is still the small bar. Most of the water a data center spends is evaporated somewhere else, by the plant making its electricity. By 2028, the direct figure alone is projected to land between 60 and 124 billion liters.1

The newer pressure

What AI adds

700,000 L

Freshwater estimated to have evaporated training GPT-3 in Microsoft’s US data centers. Training the same model in the company’s Asian data centers would have tripled it.4

4.2–6.6 bn m³

Global water withdrawal the same researchers project AI demand could account for by 2027, more than the total annual withdrawal of several mid sized countries.4

0.48 L

Water per kilowatt-hour that US data centers are expected to use on site by 2028, rising partly because liquid cooled AI hardware drinks more than the racks it replaces.1
